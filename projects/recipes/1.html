Pancakes

This recipe holds a very special place in my family. It became something of a tradition for my sibling and I during our juvenile days, where we would wake up early on Sunday mornings, hoping to snag just a few pancakes before the supply ran out, which usually only transpired within a few minutes. In fact, when I told them I was adding the recipe to my website for my web design design, they shared this anecdote with me:
These pancakes are actually so good. They're delicious in a way that only homemade pancakes feel, and that's what makes them so special.
-WH
Here's the thing: The formula seems so basic, yet it manages to melt in your mouth like the best thing you've ever tasted. It was the feeling I only but yearned for as a kid, and I can't wait to share it with you in this straightforward yet delectable step-by-step recipe.

Ingredients Recipe
  • 1 1/2 cups sifted all-purpose flour
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1 egg, well beaten
  • 1 1/4 cups milk
  • 3 tablespoons vegetable oil
  1. Sift together the flour, baking powder, salt, and sugar into a large bowl.
  2. Combine egg, milk, and oil in a medium bowl.
  3. Add the wet mixture to the dry mixture and stir until blended.
  4. Heat griddle over moderately high heat (about 350° F).
  5. Grease griddle lightly.
  6. Pour a scant 1/4 cup badder onto the griddle.
  7. Bake until the center is full of unbroken bubbles.
  8. Turn with a broad spatula and brown other side.
Makes about 12 5-inch pancakes.
Tips, Tricks, and & Substitutions
  • A good way to know if the griddle is at the right temperature is that if a few drops of cold water poured on will dance in small beads before evaporating.
  • You can use buttermilk or sour milk instead. Substitute 1/2 teaspoon baking soda for 1/2 teaspoon of the baking powder.
  • To make the pancakes richer, you can add an extra egg and another 1/4 cup of milk.
